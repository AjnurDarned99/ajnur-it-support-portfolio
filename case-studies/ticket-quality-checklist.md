# Practice Guide: What Makes a Useful Support Ticket?

A good ticket helps another technician understand the problem without repeating every question.

## Checklist

- **Summary:** short and specific, for example "Cannot open shared drive" rather than "It doesn't work".
- **Scope:** one user/device or multiple users? Which service is affected?
- **Impact:** what work is blocked and how urgent is it?
- **Timing:** when did the problem start? Include time zone when relevant.
- **Evidence:** exact error text/code, steps to reproduce, and safe screenshots if permitted.
- **Checks:** each test and its observed result, in chronological order.
- **Resolution or next step:** what changed, whether the user confirmed success, and who owns the next action.
- **Security:** redact personal data. Never include passwords, MFA codes, session tokens or full payment-card details.

## Weak vs. useful example

**Weak:** "Login broken. Please fix."

**Better (fictional):** "User A cannot complete sign-in to the internal portal in Chrome. Issue began around 09:10 CET. Error shown: `Access denied (reference ABC-123)`. Checked approved service status and reproduced once. No credentials were requested or recorded. Next action: application/identity team to review the reference."

The details above are fictional placeholders for practice, not a real incident.
